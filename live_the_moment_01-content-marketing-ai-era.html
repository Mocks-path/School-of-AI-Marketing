Match the content to the stage — where does each piece belong?

Content marketing is earning attention by being genuinely useful, not renting it with ads. But not all content does the same job — a piece that pulls in a stranger is useless for winning back a lapsed customer. Study the three funnel stages below, then drag each piece of content into the stage it serves. Guess freely — wrong pills carry a hover hint with the answer.

AWR

Awareness

Content for strangers who don't know you yet: it answers a broad question and pulls people in — the top of the funnel.

  • Blog posts & how-to guides
  • Short social videos & reels
  • SEO articles that rank on Google
CON

Consideration

Content for people weighing their options: it builds trust and shows why you're the right choice — the middle of the funnel.

  • Comparison guides & deep how-tos
  • Webinars & email newsletters
  • Case studies that prove it works
C&R

Conversion & Retention

Content for people ready to buy — and buyers you want to keep: it closes the sale and earns the next one.

  • Product demos & testimonials
  • Onboarding & how-to-use guides
  • Loyalty emails that bring buyers back
💡 Why the stage matters: when someone says "we need more content," always ask for which stage? A viral reel that never leads anyone toward a purchase, and a demo shown to people who've never heard of you, both waste effort. Matching content to the stage is what turns views into customers.

Owned or rented? — sort where your reach comes from

Marketers split media into three types, but the honest divide is simple: reach you own versus reach you rent. Owned media (and the earned attention it wins) is an asset you keep; paid media is reach you rent for as long as you pay. Read the two homes, then drag each statement to where it's true. Wrong pills hover-hint the right home.

Media you own (& earn)

The audience you keep

Your blog, website, email list, and the social following you've built — plus the earned attention it wins: shares, word of mouth, press. It takes time to build, but once it's yours it keeps working for free. This is where content marketing compounds.

Reach you rent (paid)

The audience you borrow

Google Ads, Instagram promotions, sponsored posts. Instant reach the moment you pay — great for a fast push. But it stops the second the budget stops: you rent the attention, you never own it, and every new visit costs again.

Media you own (& earn)
You keep it · it compounds
Reach you rent (paid)
You borrow it · it stops when you stop paying
💡 Most real businesses use both: paid ads for a fast push today, and owned content that compounds quietly for years. The mistake is renting reach forever — the day you stop paying, paid reach vanishes, while a blog post or email list you own keeps earning attention long after it was made.
