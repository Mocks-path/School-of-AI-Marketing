Technical SEO: plumbing, links, and proof.

You have keywords, content, and a site. Now comes the part that separates "published a page" from "ranking on Google": making sure search engines can actually crawl and index you, earning links that vouch for you, and reading the numbers without kidding yourself. Fair warning — SEO is a months-long game with no guaranteed rankings, and that's fine, because the compounding is worth the wait.

01 / 04

Fix the foundation or chase links? Pick with your data, not your gut

Great content still won't rank if Google can't crawl or index it, or if nobody links to you. Technical SEO and authority are the two halves of the foundation. Neither is "better." The right first move depends on one blunt question about whether search engines can even see your site.

Technical SEO is the plumbing: crawlability (can bots reach your pages?), indexing (did Google actually store them?), your XML sitemap and robots.txt, site speed and Core Web Vitals, mobile-friendliness, and HTTPS. It's unglamorous but decisive — if a page can't be crawled or is accidentally blocked, no amount of good writing will save it. Fixing technical issues is often the fastest win a beginner can get.

Authority (backlinks — other trustworthy sites linking to yours) is Google's word-of-mouth. A link from a respected site is a vote of confidence, and it is slow, unpredictable, and outside your direct control. You might pitch twenty journalists and hear back from one. But authority compounds: earn a strong link and it keeps passing trust for years. Never buy links — Google penalises paid link schemes, and it can tank a site you spent months building.

The best beginner play is usually neither extreme: fix the crawl-and-index basics first, then earn links ethically. The moment a technical audit is clean — pages indexed, fast, mobile-friendly — pour your energy into digital PR and genuinely useful content that people want to link to. Let the foundation carry the authority.

The one mistake this tree prevents

Beginners chase backlinks because links feel like "real SEO," while Google is quietly failing to index half their pages — effort spent, nothing ranking. If the honest answer to question one is "yes, problems," fixing the technical foundation isn't the boring chore. It's the correct first move, and often the fastest win you'll get.

02 / 04

Anatomy of a technical SEO audit

A good technical audit — the kind that finds why pages won't rank — follows the same five stages almost every time: crawl, index, speed, architecture, fix. Learn the flow, and AI can help you work through it far faster.

The single most important idea in technical SEO: crawlability comes before everything. If Google's bot can't reach a page — blocked by robots.txt, hidden behind a broken link, or stuck on a redirect loop — nothing else matters, because that page effectively doesn't exist in search. That first stage is the crawl, and pros always confirm it before touching anything downstream.

After the crawl, the audit follows a reliable arc: confirm pages are actually indexed, check speed and Core Web Vitals, review site architecture and internal links, and finish by prioritising the fixes — most impactful first.

Where does AI fit? It's your analyst and triage partner, not your crawler. A tool like Screaming Frog does the crawling; AI reads the export, clusters similar issues, drafts a plain-English fix list, and suggests internal links between related pages. A human still decides what ships, because AI can misread context or over-flag harmless things.

03 / 04

Search Console: the numbers that tell you the truth

Google Search Console gives you four numbers for every page and query: impressions, clicks, CTR, and average position. Clicks ÷ impressions × 100 is your CTR. A page can rack up thousands of impressions and still send you almost nobody — a "good-sounding" ranking can quietly be doing nothing.

That's because impressions aren't visits. A page ranking at position 8 barely gets seen; move it to position 3 and clicks can multiply. So every page has a rough expected CTR for its position: position × CTR curve tells you whether a page is under- or over-performing. A page at position 3 pulling 1% CTR is underperforming — a better title and meta description could double its clicks without ranking a single spot higher.

Here is the part most beginners skip: SEO takes months, and there are no guaranteed rankings. That's normal. The skill isn't hitting page one overnight — it's a monthly routine where you decide, with rules written down, when to refresh a page, scale a winner, or kill a dead one. Two useful rules of thumb (all figures below are illustrative):

Refresh rule — impressions, no clicks: if a page gets thousands of impressions but almost no clicks, ranking is fine but the title/snippet is dull. Rewrite the title and meta description, then retest.
Kill-or-scale rule: a page climbing in position and clicks month over month gets more internal links and expansion (scale); a page stuck at page three for months with no movement gets pruned or merged (kill). Sunk effort isn't a strategy.

Reading the verdict like a pro

The verdict compares a page's performance to its expected CTR for that position, not to some magic number. Far below expectation? Prune or rewrite — clinging to a dead page is not a strategy. Hovering near the line? That's the interesting zone: a sharper title, a better meta description, or a few internal links can tip it over. Comfortably above? Expand the winner patiently — rankings build over months, not overnight, and there are never guarantees.

04 / 04

The 7-step monthly SEO routine

SEO reporting fails from vagueness, not difficulty. Here is one monthly routine, one job per step, with a clear "done" line for each — so you always know whether this month's review actually happened.

Set expectations first: the goal of one month is not a page-one ranking. The goal is a complete, repeatable loop — technical health checked, metrics pulled, wins scaled, losers pruned, a decision made — plus honest data about what's moving. SEO takes months and guarantees nothing. The people who win are the ones whose routine is small, consistent, and documented.

Step 1

Run a technical health check

▸
  • Crawl the site with Screaming Frog; check for broken links, redirect chains, and noindex tags
  • Confirm the XML sitemap and robots.txt are correct in Search Console
  • Spot-check Core Web Vitals and mobile-friendliness on your key pages

Paste the crawl export into AI and ask it to flag the top technical issues in plain English. Verify each one before acting.

Done when

No blocking crawl/index errors on key pages, and a short list of any real issues to fix.

Step 2

Pull the Search Console numbers

▸
  • Export impressions, clicks, CTR, and average position for the last 28 days
  • Compare against the previous period — note what rose and what fell
  • Cross-check ranking movements in a rank tracker (Ahrefs or Semrush)
AI assist

Feed AI the Search Console export; ask for the top 5 rising and top 5 falling queries, and a plausible reason for each.

Done when

You have this month's four core metrics saved, with clear notes on the biggest movers.

Step 3

Fix the top 3 technical issues

▸
  • Take the highest-impact issues from Step 1 and fix them first
  • Speed wins: compress images, defer scripts, lean on PageSpeed Insights
  • Re-submit fixed pages for indexing and confirm they recover
AI assist

Triage and plain-English fix lists are AI's home turf. The judgement call on what to actually change is yours — verify before you ship.

Done when

The three priority issues are fixed and re-checked, with before/after scores noted.

Step 4

Tidy internal links & architecture

▸
  • Cluster related pages into topics and link them to each other
  • Add links from strong pages to important ones sitting too deep
  • Write your refresh / scale / kill rules down BEFORE you touch content
AI assist

Ask AI to group your pages into topic clusters and suggest internal links — a fast way to spread authority sensibly.

Done when

Related pages link to each other, buried pages have a route in, and your decision rules are written where you can't ignore them.

Step 5

Read the data — prune the losers

▸
  • Thousands of impressions but almost no clicks? Rewrite the title and snippet
  • Stuck deep on page three for months with no movement? Prune or merge it
  • Do NOT touch anything borderline — give it one more month of data first
AI assist

Paste each page's impressions, clicks, CTR, and position; ask AI to sort them into refresh / watch / winner with reasons.

Done when

Every key page has a written verdict, and dead pages are actually pruned or merged — not "monitored."

Step 6

Refresh the near-misses

▸
  • Take a page ranking on the edge of page one and update it — fresh info, better title
  • Improve the meta description and add 2–3 internal links to it
  • Earn one ethical link if you can — a genuine pitch, never a bought link
AI assist

Tell AI which page under-performs its position; ask for a sharper title, meta description, and a short list of sections to add.

Done when

The refreshed page is republished, internally linked, and logged so next month's data is a clean comparison.

Step 7

Decide: scale, refresh, or move on

▸
  • Page climbing in position and clicks? Scale it — expand and add internal links
  • Close to page one with a clear fix? Queue one more refresh next month
  • No movement anywhere after months? Prune it and redirect the effort
AI assist

Have AI write the monthly summary: what rose, what fell, and the top 3 hypotheses to test next month.

Done when

A decision is made and written down. "Let's see how it goes" is not one of the three options.

What a "successful" first month really looks like

A genuinely good month one: you fixed two indexing errors, sped up your slowest pages, refreshed one near-miss title that lifted its CTR, pruned two dead pages, and confirmed your best guide finally getting indexed. Rankings barely moved yet — and that's fine. The loop works, the foundation is cleaner, and next month starts from there, not from zero. SEO compounds; patience is the strategy.

What to carry forward

Rankings are earned through crawlable, fast pages and honest links — never bought. A page can't rank if Google can't crawl or index it. Search Console metrics only mean something next to a page's expected CTR. And SEO is a months-long compounding game with no guarantees. Run the routine, read the numbers, make the call.

  • Technical foundation first: crawlability, indexing, sitemap, robots.txt, speed, mobile, HTTPS
  • Architecture & internal links spread authority; AI clusters pages and suggests links
  • Authority = earned backlinks. Build link-worthy assets and do digital PR — never buy links
  • Measure with Search Console: impressions, clicks, CTR, average position + rank tracking
  • Monthly loop = check tech, pull data, refresh / scale / kill. Patience beats guarantees
