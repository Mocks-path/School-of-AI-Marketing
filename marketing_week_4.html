Stage Two · Content Creation

AI Content Ideas — Not Every Post Idea Is Worth Making

A good content idea has a checkable profile: it fits the brand's voice, it gives the viewer a reason to stop scrolling, and it has a hook — a reason to care in the first second. AI can score every idea so your best effort goes to the posts most likely to land.

📖 The story so far Tuesday. You described Crumb & Co to ChatGPT in plain English — "warm, cosy neighbourhood bakery, sourdough and cinnamon rolls, small-batch, run by hand, a little imperfect and proud of it." It helped you brainstorm 20 content ideas. But 20 is too many to make equally. Three ideas below landed on your shortlist. Inspect each, then meet the Idea-Score Lab and learn to sort scroll-stoppers from slop.
🥐
On-brand · Strong hook

"5am sourdough" Reel

A 20-second Reel of dough being shaped by hand at dawn, real hands, real flour. On-brand, native to the platform, and a hook people stop for. Small idea, exactly the bakery's kind of post.

🏢
Off-brand · Copycat

Trending dance (no baking)

A big viral dance trend everyone's doing. Might rack up views, but it says nothing about the bakery. Impressive numbers for a day — brings zero real fans of the bread.

👻
Generic · No hook

"Buy our bread" graphic

A plain stock-photo graphic with the logo and "Fresh bread daily!" No story, no hook, no reason to stop. It looks like an ad, so the scroll never slows down.

Stage Three · The Caption & Hook

Write Hooks & Captions People Actually Save — AI Drafts, You Direct

The average scroll gives a post well under 2 seconds — almost always lost because the first line is about the brand, not the viewer. The whole game is one true, specific hook in the brand's own voice. AI can write it — but only if you feed it the real story.

📖 The story so far Wednesday. You paste Crumb & Co's story and photos into ChatGPT and ask for a caption. Its first draft is generic slop — "Delicious freshly baked goods await you!" Useless. So you direct it: audience (neighbours who love a cosy find), the moment (dough shaped by hand at 5am), and a genuine, specific detail in the bakery's warm voice. Try the three voices below and watch the caption rewrite itself.

Stage Four · Schedule & Post

Post Consistently Without Burning Out — Build the Week

One post is not a strategy — growth comes from showing up week after week at the right times. But cramming is dangerous: post ten things in one day then vanish, and the feed forgets you. You'll batch and schedule a whole week and respect the rhythm that keeps the algorithm delivering.

That's exactly how small brands go quiet and get forgotten by Friday. Stage Five · Community & Engagement

Turn Comments into Community — and Never Let a Fan Go Ignored

A comment is not the end — it's the start of the real relationship, and exactly where small brands quietly lose fans. The fix is a system: AI drafts warm replies, you keep them human, and you log every conversation so nobody gets forgotten.

📖 The story so far Friday morning. The 5am Reel took off a little. Comments are trickling in. A neighbour asked "do you do gluten-free?" Two people tagged friends. Someone DM'd "can I pre-order a loaf?" This is the moment small brands blow it — a warm comment gets buried under a busy bake and left on read. You won't. Work the community-and-engagement checklist, and watch the conversation come to life in the feed.

Your community & engagement checklist

  • Reply to the "gluten-free?" comment with a  warm, human answer  — no copy-paste template
  • AI-draft friendly replies to the easy comments, then you edit for voice
  • Answer the "can I pre-order?" DM personally and log it
  • Do 10 minutes of social listening — see what neighbours ask for
  • Set a daily reminder so no comment or DM sits unanswered > 24h
  • Thank and re-share the customers who tagged the bakery
✅ The community is alive. Every comment has a real reply, and nothing is sitting on read. This is what "shows up for its fans" actually looks like.
