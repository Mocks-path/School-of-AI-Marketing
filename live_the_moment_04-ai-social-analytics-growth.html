1

Read your analytics in order — start with the goal, end with the decision

Reading social analytics well is a sequence, not a stare at a dashboard. It starts with your goal ("what was this post for?"), then you check the metrics that actually match that goal, and finish with a decision about what to do next. Skip the goal and every number looks equally important — which means none of them are. Click the steps in the order a monthly review should follow. A wrong click nudges you.

◎

Start With the Goal

The "what for?" — every post has a job: reach new people, spark engagement, or drive clicks. Name the goal first, because it decides which numbers matter.

+ Makes the right metric obvious − No goal = every number looks equal
▤

Meaningful Metrics

The "did it work?" — engagement rate, saves and shares, watch time, follower growth, clicks. These track real interest, not just eyeballs. Illustrative, not guaranteed.

+ Show genuine interest and action − Vanity likes alone can mislead you
⇅

The Decision

The "so what now?" — double-down on what worked, iterate on what nearly worked, or drop what flopped. One post is a hint; a pattern over weeks is the real signal.

+ Turns numbers into next steps − One data point isn't a strategy
💡 Read any review as a sentence: "Goal was reach, so I check impressions and follower growth, then compare to my usual, then decide to double-down, iterate, or drop." Build exactly that flow below.

From goal to metric — match each goal to the number that proves it

A metric only means something once you know what you were trying to do. Each marketing goal has one number that actually tells you whether it worked — reading the wrong one is how people fool themselves with vanity stats. 3

Real growth, or a shady shortcut? — the judgment call

Some growth tactics compound slowly and honestly — better hooks, series, collaborations, showing up consistently. Others buy a quick spike and quietly rot the account: bought followers, engagement pods, follow/unfollow games. Getting this line right is what separates an audience that actually cares from a big empty number. Judge each tactic: does it grow honestly, or is it a shady shortcut?
