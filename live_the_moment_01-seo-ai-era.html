Four hands-on activities. Sort real searches by the intent behind them, click a page through how engines crawl, index, and rank it, bust six SEO myths, then do the math that separates ranking from real traffic.

1

Sort the search — Informational, Commercial, or Navigational?

A keyword is not just words — it's a job the searcher wants done. The question that separates every query: what does this person actually want right now? Study the three cards, then drag each real search into the right intent bucket. Guess freely — wrong pills carry a hover hint with the answer.

IN

Informational

The searcher wants to learn something, not buy yet. "How", "what", "why", "guide", "meaning". Answer it well and you earn trust — and often an AI Overview citation.

+ Huge volume, builds topical authority + Perfect for blog posts & guides − Rarely converts to a sale on the spot − Increasingly answered by AI, zero-click
CO

Commercial / Transactional

The searcher is ready to act or buy — comparing, pricing, or looking for "best", "buy", "near me", "coupon". These pages carry the money.

+ High conversion, real revenue intent + Worth targeting with product pages − Fierce competition, lower volume − Needs strong authority to rank
NA

Navigational

The searcher already knows where they want to go — a specific brand, login, or site. "MokshPath login", "YouTube", "Semrush pricing".

+ Easy wins if it's your own brand + Signals real brand demand − Almost impossible to steal a rival's brand − Little value unless the brand is yours
💡 Why this matters in the AI era: AI can draft content and cluster keywords in seconds — but matching a page to the true intent behind a query is still human judgment. Google and AI Overviews reward pages that answer the real question. Name the intent first; add AI second.

💡 The honest takeaway: ranking is not traffic. Position, CTR, and the growing zero-click share (AI Overviews answering right on the page) each shrink the funnel — so the biggest levers are climbing from #3 toward #1 and targeting keywords AI can't fully answer. That's why the next lessons obsess over intent, content quality, and Search Console data, not chasing a single "#1 ranking".
