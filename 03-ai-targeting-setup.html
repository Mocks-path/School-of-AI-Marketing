Week 2 · Lesson 03 · AI Ads

A brilliant ad shown to the wrong people sells nothing.

In paid ads, you don't wait for buyers — you pay to put your ad in front of them. But in front of whom? The old way was to hand-pick interests and hope. The new way is to let the platform's AI hunt for the people most likely to buy, guided by the tracking data you feed it. This lesson turns AI into your audience-finder, campaign architect and always-on optimiser — in plain English, no jargon required.

01 / 04

Audiences come in three temperatures

Before you spend a rupee, you need to know who you're paying to reach. Marketers sort every audience by how ready-to-buy they are — cold, warm and hot — because a stranger and a repeat visitor need completely different ads. Match the temperature to the message and your money works harder.

Beginners try to sell to everyone the same way: one ad, blasted at the whole world, asking cold strangers to "Buy now!". It rarely works. A cold audience has never heard of you — they need to be introduced. A warm audience has engaged (watched a video, visited the site) — they need a reason to come back. A hot audience added to cart or is a past buyer — they just need a nudge. Your audience is a bridge between a person's readiness and the right message. Every audience type is a chance to spend wisely or waste money.

Cold introduces, warm reminds, hot closes

The art of good targeting is matching the message to the temperature. A cold stranger needs a friendly introduction, not a "last chance!" discount; a hot cart-abandoner doesn't need to be told who you are — they need one small nudge to finish. Show the wrong ad to the wrong temperature and you burn budget: begging strangers to buy, or annoying loyal customers with a beginner's pitch. Custom audiences (people who already interacted with you) and lookalikes (strangers who resemble your buyers) let you sort people by temperature automatically. Your job is to give each group the message it actually needs.

02 / 04

Now the algorithm finds the buyers for you

The most nerve-wracking part of paid ads used to be guessing exactly which interests and demographics to target. Today the platforms have flipped it: with Meta Advantage+ Audience and Google Performance Max, you give the AI a goal and it hunts across billions of signals to find the people most likely to buy. Your role shifts from hand-picker to guide.

Here's what the AI does genuinely well: it reads who has bought and engaged before, then finds more people like them automatically, tests hundreds of audience combinations you'd never try by hand, and shifts spend toward whatever is working — in near real time. The mistake beginners make with AI targeting

The instinct is to over-restrict: pile on ten interests, narrow the age range to a sliver, exclude everyone. That starves the algorithm of room to learn and drives your cost per result up. The winning move with modern AI targeting is to go broad and let it optimise — give it your country, a wide age band, and one or two seed interests, then feed it good conversion data and get out of its way. The AI needs a big pool to test against. Trust it to narrow down where you'd only guess. Start broad, watch what converts, then refine.

03 / 04

Campaign structure, in plain English

Every ads platform uses the same three nesting boxes. A campaign holds your goal (like "get sales"). Inside it, an ad set (Meta) or ad group (Google) holds your audience and budget. Inside that, the ad is the actual image and words people see. Master these three and the whole dashboard stops being scary.

Think of it as a filing cabinet: the campaign is the drawer (your goal), the ad set is a folder inside it (who you're targeting and how much you'll spend), and the ad is the single page in that folder (the creative). As a beginner, keep it simple — one campaign, one ad set, a couple of ads. Don't build twenty ad sets on day one; that splits your budget so thin the AI never learns. Daily vs lifetime budget, and who holds it

Two budget decisions trip up beginners. First, daily vs lifetime: a daily budget spends a set amount every day (predictable, easy to start with); a lifetime budget spends a total across a date range and lets the AI spend more on your best days. Second, where the budget lives: put it on each ad set yourself, or hand it to the campaign and let the AI split it — that's CBO (Campaign Budget Optimisation), now called Advantage campaign budget on Meta. As a beginner, start with a daily budget and let the platform optimise. You can always take back control later.

04 / 04

Tracking is the fuel your AI runs on

Here's the part beginners skip and later regret: the AI can only find buyers if you tell it what a buyer looks like. That signal comes from conversion tracking — the Meta Pixel, the Conversions API and the events they report (view, add-to-cart, purchase). No tracking, no fuel; the algorithm is flying blind.

Think about what the AI is trying to do: match your ad to the person most likely to complete your goal. It learns from every event your tracking sends back — who added to cart, who actually bought. The more clean, honest conversion data it gets, the smarter it becomes. You can't fake sales, but you fully control the tracking setup. Your first-campaign checklist, in order

Build it in this sequence and nothing gets missed: install the Pixel and, if you can, connect the Conversions API (server-side tracking that survives iOS blocking). Confirm your purchase and add-to-cart events fire on a test order. Pick one clear objective (usually Sales). Choose a broad audience and let Advantage+ / Performance Max optimise. Set a daily budget you can afford to lose for a week. Add a consent banner so you collect data legally. Then leave it alone for about 7 days to finish learning — resist the urge to tinker daily. Simple, honest, patient: that's the whole recipe.

What to carry forward

You now know that in paid ads, the modern job isn't hand-picking every buyer — it's guiding an AI that finds them for you. Sort audiences by temperature, hand the platform a clear goal and a real budget, and above all feed it clean conversion data — tracking is the fuel. Keep the structure simple as a beginner, respect privacy and consent, and give the algorithm a week to learn before you judge it. AI does the finding and optimising; you set the guardrails and keep it honest. Next lesson: writing the ad creative that makes all this targeting pay off.
