Week 5 · Lesson 03 · AI Email

An email is a tiny stage with one job.

A great marketing email is not a wall of words — it's a handful of small parts that each have to earn the next click. The from-name earns the open. The subject line and preview text earn the read. The body earns trust. The one call-to-action earns the click. Get those parts right and even a small list outperforms a big blast. This lesson shows how to write each part with AI — subject lines, brand-voice body copy, personalization that actually fits the reader — and how to land in the inbox instead of the spam folder.

01 / 04

The anatomy of an email that gets opened

Before you write a single word, know what a reader actually sees. In the inbox they see three things — your from-name, your subject line, and the preview text peeking after it. Those decide the open. Inside, the body and one clear call-to-action decide the click. Relevance is what ties it all together: the same email means nothing to a champion and everything to a lapsed subscriber. Click a segment to see how one message gets re-shaped for each reader.

The mistake beginners make is writing "the email" — one message blasted to everyone. But a subscriber who bought last week, a loyal repeat reader, and someone who has gone quiet for months are three different people opening three different inboxes. Personalization that matters isn't sticking their first name in the subject line — it's segment relevance: sending the champion a thank-you and early access, the at-risk reader a genuine reason to come back, the new subscriber a warm welcome. AI groups your list by behaviour and helps you write a fitting version of the email for each. Click each group to see who they are and what the email should say.

Why segments beat blasts

Send the same "20% off everything" email to all 500 subscribers and you annoy your champions (who'd have happily read a normal update) while barely moving the dormant ones (who forgot who you are). Segment first, and the champions get an early-access note, the going-quiet readers get a genuine "we miss you" with one strong reason to return, the new subscribers get a warm welcome that sets expectations. Same effort, far better results — because relevance, not volume, is what earns the open. (Illustrative figures.)

The subject line that lands in spam

You can write a perfect email and still never be seen — because the subject line tripped a spam filter, or the reader's thumb skipped past it. Both risks are predictable. AI can score a draft before you send and tell you exactly what to fix. Toggle the common mistakes below and watch the risk gauge climb.

Deliverability isn't luck; it's a checklist. Spam filters and tired readers react to the same tells — ALL-CAPS shouting, "FREE!!!" and money words, a wall of image with no text, a dozen exclamation marks, sketchy sending setup. The AI has seen millions of emails that got flagged or ignored, so it can read a draft the way a filter and a reader would and flag the same warning signs early. The value is that you catch it before you hit send: a draft scored at 70% risk this week can be fixed in five minutes; the same draft, unchecked, quietly dies in the spam folder. SPF, DKIM & DMARC in plain English

Those three acronyms just prove the email really came from you, not an impostor. SPF lists which servers are allowed to send as your domain. DKIM adds a tamper-proof signature. DMARC tells inboxes what to do if the first two fail. Set them up once (your email tool has a guide) and your messages arrive as "verified you" instead of "suspicious stranger." One more habit: warm up a new sending address slowly — a few hundred emails a day at first, not ten thousand on day one — so inbox providers learn you're a real sender, not a spammer. The gauge flags the risk; you fix the draft and the setup before you send.

03 / 04

Writing subject lines with AI

The subject line is the hardest 40 characters in marketing — and the easiest place for AI to help. Ask for ten, keep the two good ones. But you still need to know a good line from a bad one. AI scores each draft on clarity, curiosity, and honesty, then explains the fix. Click a candidate to see its score explained.

Good subject lines follow simple frameworks: curiosity ("The one setting we changed"), benefit ("Cut your prep time in half"), specificity (a real number or name beats a vague promise), and honesty (the line must match what's inside — clickbait kills trust and, over time, deliverability). Feed AI your goal and a couple of details, ask for a batch, and it drafts in every framework at once. Then judge them: a high score means clear + honest + a reason to open; a low score means vague, hypey, or misleading. Click each candidate to see the AI's reasoning and how to improve it.

Preview text: the free second subject line

Right after the subject, the inbox shows a slice of preview text — and most senders waste it by leaving it blank (so it shows "View in browser" or raw code) or by repeating the subject. Treat it as a second subject line: use it to extend the promise. Subject: "The prep step you're skipping." Preview: "It takes 30 seconds and saves your whole evening." Together they earn the open. Ask AI to write the subject and preview as a matched pair, keep the two best, and — always — read them yourself before sending. AI drafts fast, but the final judgment (and any facts or numbers) is yours to check.

04 / 04

A repeatable email-writing workflow

Everything so far becomes one habit you can run in fifteen minutes: pick the segment, brief the AI, draft the parts, score and fix, then send yourself a test. Do it in this order every time and writing an email stops being a blank-page ordeal. Watch the workflow build, step by step.

Here's how the parts fit into one motion. First you choose the reader — which segment, and the one thing you want them to do. Then you brief the AI with your goal, brand voice, and a few real details. It drafts the pieces — from-name, ten subject lines, matched preview text, a short skimmable body, one CTA. You score and cut: keep the best subject, tighten the body to sound like you, check every fact and number by hand. Finally you send a test to yourself and read it on your phone before it ever reaches the list. Same steps, every send — the blank page becomes a checklist.

Why this matters for you

This is where AI stops being a novelty and becomes a reliable teammate. A vague "write me an email" gives you generic mush; this ordered workflow gives you a message that fits the reader, sounds like your brand, and clears the spam filter — in a fraction of the time. What to carry forward

A great email is a handful of small parts, each earning the next click: from-name, subject, preview text, body, one CTA. AI helps you write every part faster — but relevance beats volume, and a human pass is non-negotiable. Segment first so the message fits the reader, score subject lines for clarity and honesty, keep the body in your voice and free of spam tells, and always test on your own phone before you send. Respect the inbox and it will keep letting you in.
